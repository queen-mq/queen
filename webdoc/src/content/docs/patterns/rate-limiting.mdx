An SMS provider accepts five messages a second for each customer. Send more and it refuses them, or blocks the account. The messages must still all go out, in order.

A stream with a gate decides, message by message, whether a message may go on now. The token bucket gate keeps its state per partition, in the broker: here five tokens per customer, refilled at five a second. A message the gate refuses stops its partition there, and comes back, with the ones after it, when its lease ends.

<SdkCode id="uc-rate" />

Five messages went at once, and the rest followed at about five a second, so the twelfth left about two seconds after the first: all twelve, in order.

<Fig id="sms-gate" caption="The token bucket gate: 5, 5, then 2, in order. A refill a few milliseconds short lets 4 through, not 5." />

<Why>the bucket lives in the broker, per customer, and survives restarts: no Redis next to it. Waiting messages stay in their queue, in order, and never need a queue of your own.</Why>

One thing to know: the refused messages wait for their lease, so size the two together. The most a gate can let through is the smaller of `refillPerSec` and `capacity` divided by the lease in seconds, so keep `capacity` near `refillPerSec` times the lease.
