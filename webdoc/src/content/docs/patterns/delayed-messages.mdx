A day after an order is delivered, ask for a review. If the delivery is late, move the reminder. If the guest already wrote a review, cancel it. The usual answer is a cron job that scans a table every minute.

A timer is a message that waits. It is named by its queue and a key: scheduling the same key again moves it, and cancelling the key removes it. When it is due, it arrives in its queue as a normal message, through the log.

<Fig id="timer-reminder" caption="A keyed timer: schedule, move, cancel." />

<SdkCode id="uc-reminder" />

The first call answered `scheduled` and the second `rescheduled`; after the cancel, no reminder was left. A short timer of the same queue arrived as a message, with its payload as data.

<Why>the key makes a reminder easy to move or cancel, and a retried schedule is harmless. Put the timer in the [transaction](/writing-applications/transactions/) of the step that creates it, and it exists exactly when the step committed.</Why>

One thing to know: a cancel can arrive just after the timer fired. The consumer of the reminder should check the order's state before it sends anything.
