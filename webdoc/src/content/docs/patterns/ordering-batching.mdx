A chat: every conversation must be handled in order, and a slow one must not delay the others. And a guest rarely sends one message. They send three in a row: "Hi!", "Is breakfast included?", "And is there parking?". An assistant that answers each message at once answers three times.

Every conversation is a partition of its own, created by its first message, so its messages stay in order and it waits on nobody else. `windowBuffer` adds the batching: new messages of a conversation wait until the first of them is two seconds old, and then go to the handler together.

<Fig id="chat-burst" caption="A conversation's burst waits two seconds, then goes as one batch." />

<SdkCode id="uc-chat" />

The handler ran once, two seconds after the first message, with the three texts.

<Why>a conversation comes in bursts, not one message at a time. Waiting a moment gives the assistant the whole question. Each conversation waits only for its own burst, never for the others.</Why>

One thing to know: the window ends two seconds after the oldest waiting message, even if the guest keeps typing, so a long burst is answered in parts, every two seconds.
