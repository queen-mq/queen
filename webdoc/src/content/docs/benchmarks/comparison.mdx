Queen, Kafka, RabbitMQ and pgmq ran the same twelve-stage ordered pipeline on one machine, under
the same CPU and memory limits, at four shapes. The design differences behind these numbers are on
[Comparison](/start/compare). Three of the four are not systems we run every day, so every knob we
touched is recorded with the run.

## The rig

Two machines, both **32 vCPU / 62 GB**, Intel Xeon Platinum 8358 at 2.60 GHz, NVMe, KVM, kernel
6.8.0-136, Docker 29.7.1. One runs the system under test, the other only the load generator, which
never exceeded about 13% of its 32 cores.

**Resource parity was a fix, not a given.** The compose files for pgmq, Kafka and RabbitMQ carried
`cpus: 8` and `memory: 16g` while Queen's carried no limits at all. Every system below runs
`CM_CPUS=32 CM_MEM=56g`, with Kafka's heap scaled to 16 GB in proportion.

PostgreSQL was resized for the box (`shared_buffers` 16 GB, `effective_cache_size` 45 GB,
`max_wal_size` 32 GB, `max_connections` 600) with **`synchronous_commit = on` and `fsync = on` left
untouched**, so Queen and pgmq both pay a real fsync per commit and Kafka, at its defaults, does
not.

CPU is the mean over the active window from 1 Hz cgroup samples, memory is peak RSS across the
system's containers, disk is mean host write throughput.

## The workload

A twelve-stage channel-manager pipeline: an ingress event fans out to six deliveries across two
chained consumer hops, with simulated per-message work of 10 to 20 ms on the first hop and 10 to
30 ms downstream. Ordering is per property, checked on every run.

**Cardinality** is the number of ordered lanes. **Density** is messages per second per lane.

## Sparse: 2 000 ev/s over 1 000 lanes

12 000 deliveries per second demanded, 2 per second per lane. Every system served the full rate.

| system | p50 ms | p95 ms | deliveries/s | cores | RAM | disk MB/s | physical queues | consumers |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **pgmq** | **55.1** | **65.5** | 11 700 | 6.5 | 2.2 GB | 65 | 12 | 96 |
| **Queen** | 71.5 | 92.7 | 11 700 | 7.4 | **1.8 GB** | **51** | **4** | 48 |
| **RabbitMQ** | 92.7 | 120.2 | 11 699 | 7.0 | 2.6 GB | 62 | **12 000** | **12 000** |
| **Kafka** | 142.9 | 170.0 | 11 700 | **2.2** | 11.6 GB | **15** | 4 | 48 |

All PASS: zero gaps, zero order violations, zero duplicates. Single run each.

pgmq wins this shape and not narrowly: at this density there is nothing to amortise, and its
stateless re-derivation costs less than Queen's ring. Kafka is a third of everyone's CPU and the
slowest here, on 11.6 GB against Queen's 1.8 GB. RabbitMQ needs 12 000 queues and 12 000 consumers
to express what Queen expresses with 4 and 48.

## Dense: 12 000 ev/s over 1 000 lanes

72 000 deliveries per second demanded, 12 per second per lane. Only two systems kept up.

| system | p50 ms | p95 ms | deliveries/s | % of rate | cores | RAM | disk MB/s | shed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Queen** | **680** (range 340 to 3 234) | 881.7 | **69 110** | **96%** | 16.0 | 4.5 GB | 77 | **0** |
| **Kafka** | 2 287 | 5 439 | 64 252 | 89% | **3.9** | 12.2 GB | **22** | 0 |
| **pgmq** | 11 863 | 16 777 | 45 622 | 63% | 19.3 | 5.1 GB | 111 | 104 780 |
| **RabbitMQ** | 19 952 | 39 903 | 19 666 | **27%** | 9.7 | 2.7 GB | 162 | 363 942 |

All PASS on correctness.

<Aside type="caution">
**Queen's figure is a median of eight runs, not a best.** The same cell, same build, same
configuration, produced 340, 441, 624, 680, 742, 1 049, 2 966 and 3 234 ms. That 7x of dispersion is
a known defect under investigation, not measurement noise: it appears only where the admission and
lane controllers become binding, and not on sparse shapes, on the tenant scenarios or on the
three-hour soak. **Kafka, pgmq and RabbitMQ here are single runs**, so their dispersion is unknown.
</Aside>

The ranking survives that: Queen's worst run still beats pgmq and RabbitMQ, which stop serving the
offered rate and shed. Against Kafka, Queen's median is about 3x better and its range overlaps
Kafka's single observation.

**Why the reversal.** pgmq's grouped read is `GROUP BY headers->>'x-pgmq-group'` over the whole
queue table, so its cost tracks standing rows rather than group count and a backlog re-scans itself
on every read. Queen amortises over the partition visit, so more messages per visit make it cheaper
per message.

## Cardinality: 20 000 ordering keys

The same workload with 20 000 properties at 6 000 ev/s, each system running at the cardinality it
can actually express.

| system | ordered lanes | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms | cores | RAM |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **pgmq** | **240 000** | **71.5** | **881.7** | **5 439** | 15.3 | 7.0 GB |
| pgmq | 12 000 | 71.5 | 881.7 | 5 439 | 15.0 | 6.7 GB |
| **Queen** | 80 000 | 101.1 | 2 494 | 15 385 | 14.6 | 7.1 GB |
| Queen | 4 000 | 110.2 | 1 923 | 7 054 | 14.7 | 5.1 GB |
| Kafka | 800 | 142.9 | 4 194 | 8 389 | **4.6** | **1.6 GB** |
| RabbitMQ | 12 000 | 23 727 | 43 515 | 43 515 | 12.0 | 3.5 GB |

**pgmq is insensitive to cardinality**, and the two rows prove it: identical percentiles across a
twentyfold change in lane count. Its group is a value in a JSONB header, so there is no per-lane
object to scale.

**Queen's median improves with more lanes and its tail worsens**, because fewer properties contend
for one lane while the ring lap grows with lane count. **RabbitMQ at 12 000 queues** serves 57% of
the offered rate, and no tuning we applied changed that.

<Aside type="note">
These tails are this pipeline's, and it puts 10 to 30 ms of simulated work on every message across
twelve stages. A later run measured the cardinality axis on its own, with no synthetic work in the
consumers, and reached **1 000 000 ordered partitions** at p50 27.5 ms and p99 115 ms. The topology,
not the lane count, is what produces the seconds. See
[A million ordered partitions](/benchmarks/cardinality-1m).
</Aside>

### Where Kafka stops

`lanes` is partitions per topic; the harness creates four topics.

| lanes | partitions/topic | result |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 200 | 200 | p50 142.9, p95 4 988 |
| 1 000 | 1 000 | p50 155.9, p95 12 937 |
| 2 000 | 2 000 | p50 155.9, **p95 23 727** |
| 5 000 and above | 5 000+ | **topic creation refused** |

At 5 000 partitions per topic the broker returns `POLICY_VIOLATION: Request parameters do not
satisfy the configured policy`. **This is a configuration refusal on default settings, not a proven
physical limit**: our compose sets no `create.topic.policy`, so a differently configured Kafka may
go higher. The tail below that wall is not a configuration artifact: p95 grows roughly linearly with
partition count, 4 988 to 12 937 to 23 727 as partitions go 800 to 4 000 to 8 000, against 2 494 for
Queen at 80 000 ordered lanes.

## Ordered-consume ceiling, raw

The tables above measure a topology. This measures raw ordered consumption, with a purpose-built
loader driving pgmq's own API directly (`send_batch` / `read_grouped_head` / `delete`), 1 000
groups, 256-byte payloads.

| target msg/s | readers | pop/s across the run | final queue depth | verdict |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| 20 000 | 4 | does not keep up | 685 593 | ✗ |
| **20 000** | **16** | **20 000 · 20 000 · 20 001** | **54** | **✓ p50 17 to 34 ms** |
| 20 000 | 64 | 11 803 · 3 105 · 1 333 | 1 303 551 | ✗ |
| 40 000 | 16 | 14 406 · 2 358 · 1 250 | 3 054 066 | ✗ |
| 80 000 | 16, 24, 32 | about 4 500 · 700 · 400 | about 7 000 000 | ✗ |

**pgmq's ordered-consume ceiling is between 20 000 and 40 000 msg/s on this machine.** The reader
count has a narrow optimum, because each reader is a full table scan and N readers are N scans of
the same table. With one group per queue, pgmq served 6% of a 12 000 deliveries/s target.

The push side is fine: pgmq accepted 80 000 msg/s of writes without errors. What caps is the
**ordered read**, and the failing runs show the mechanism: 767 000 standing rows gave 14 406 pop/s,
1.90 million gave 2 358, 3.06 million gave 1 250. Its plain `read()` skips the `GROUP BY` and would
go considerably faster, so the ceiling is specifically the per-key-ordered path.

**Queen's comparable figure** is this campaign's own three-hour soak, on the same machine: 600 000
msg/s sustained with leases, explicit asynchronous acks, a 60-second deduplication window and
retention active, 6.82 billion messages, zero push, pop or ack errors, flat lag, p50 120 ms. That is
15 to 30 times pgmq's ordered-consume ceiling, and pgmq has neither deduplication nor retention as
concepts. On the dedicated bench rig, at the same semantics, the figure is
[1 000 000 msg/s per side for 24 hours](/benchmarks/soak-24h).

## Correctness

**Zero gaps, zero order violations and zero duplicates in every run of the campaign**, on all four
systems, including the collapsed cells at 25 000 ev/s carrying 1.3 million messages in flight and
the runs that shed hundreds of thousands.

One correctness finding is not about Queen. pgmq's default grouped read, `read_grouped_rr`, has a
demonstrated exclusivity race under concurrent readers: an adversarial reproduction recorded 4 096
breaches in 90 seconds, against zero for `read_grouped_head` under identical load. Every pgmq figure
here uses `read_grouped_head`.

## What qualifies these numbers

Cells are 60 to 120 seconds, and Queen's instantaneous p50 drifts upward inside one because
completed-message retention outlives the run, while pgmq deletes on ack and does not drift. Relative
comparisons hold, absolute values on the longer tail do not. High-cardinality cells need longer
rated windows: at 100 000 properties the warmup delivers more messages than a 60-second window does,
so those percentiles are majority warmup.

Every cell has its `invocation.txt` with the exact command, the resource-parity environment, the
resized `postgres.conf`, image digests, machine fingerprint and 1 Hz resource samples, under
`benchmark-queen/crossbench/results/2026-08-04-scale-campaign/`. The loader is on
[Method and archive](/benchmarks/method).
