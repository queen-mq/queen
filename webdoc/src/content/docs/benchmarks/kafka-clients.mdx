Queen speaks the Kafka wire protocol from inside the broker process (`QUEEN_KAFKA_EMBEDDED=true`),
so an existing producer or consumer can point at Queen and keep its code. We measured what that
costs with the same load generator, built on franz-go, that drove Kafka itself. After a night of
work on the facade, Kafka clients see the latency Queen's own clients see, and at a million
messages a second a little better. At 1,000,000 msg/s on 200 partitions the facade's e2e p99 was
91 ms on the final build, against 146 ms for Queen's own clients (2.0.0-beta.2) and 18 ms for Kafka
4.3.1 itself; the facade ran the code of 2.0.0-beta.5 on 2026-10-02. On 2.3.0-beta.1 the same
point reads 48 ms, and the facade no longer stops consuming at 2,000,000
([2.3.0](#230-five-points)).

## The shape

The rig and load of the other pages: one topic of 200 partitions unless stated, 256-byte messages
produced 100 at a time to one partition, 60 seconds per point, figures over the last 30. The
clients were nine franz-go processes on three load machines, with `acks=all`, idempotent
producers, lz4, a 5 ms linger and classic consumer groups. The Queen cluster ran the facade on
every node, with the broker settings of the native runs. The builds are those of the facade work
that was merged as `b51e4419` (2.0.0-beta.5): `qk3` on 2026-10-01, and the final build on
2026-10-02 for the row marked so. Runs: [`benchmark-queen/2026-09-30-kafka-pulsar/runs/queen-kafka/`](https://github.com/queen-mq/queen/tree/master/benchmark-queen/2026-09-30-kafka-pulsar/runs/queen-kafka)
(`README-c3.txt` there maps tags to builds).

## 2.3.0: five points

Five points of the tables below ran again on 2026-10-13, on 2.3.0-beta.1 and on the machines of
the [2.3.0 pre-release runs](/benchmarks/methodology/#the-230-runs-on-digitalocean), to see that the release had broken nothing:
the same load generator and client settings, a fresh cluster per point. End-to-end p50 / p99 in
milliseconds, the worst load process. The machines are others of the same size, so the columns
show a direction and not an exact gain. Runs: the `b1-*` folders of the archive above.

| Point | In / out | 2.3.0-beta.1 | 2.0.0-beta.5, 2026-10-02 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300,000 msg/s, 200 partitions | 300k / 300k | 9 / 11 | 10 / 19 |
| 1,000,000, 200 partitions | 1,000k / 1,000k | 12 / 48 | 35 / 91 |
| 1,000,000, 10,000 partitions | 1,000k / 1,000k | 13 / 26 | 45 / 142 |
| 1,500,000, 200 partitions | 1,499k / 1,498k | 98 / 175 | 185 / 3,785 |
| 2,000,000, 200 partitions | 2,007k / 1,992k | 334 / 20,578 | 1.91M in, 105k out |

No point had an error or shed anything. At 2,000,000 msg/s the consumers now keep the rate. Seven
of the nine load processes end at 220 / 350 ms; two hold consumers that fall steadily behind, to
18 and 25 seconds in the last window, which is where the p99 of that row comes from.

Kafka itself on these machines read 8 / 11 ms at 1,000,000 msg/s on 100 partitions, and Queen's
own clients 10 / 19 ms.

The gap that grows with partitions is on the fetch path. The facade answers Fetch up to version 6,
below fetch sessions (KIP-227), so every fetch names all of its consumer's partitions, and at
100,000 partitions the nine client processes used 22 cores against Queen and 6 against Kafka. We
left sessions out on purpose: a session is per-connection state, and the facade keeps none, so it
can restart the way a Kafka broker does.

## What made it fast

On 2026-09-30 the same load at 1M msg/s on 200 partitions got through the facade at 935,000 in and
924,000 out, with an e2e of 2.0 s at p50 and 3.8 s at p99 (run `qk-1x200-1000k`). Four changes,
all in `b51e4419`, took that to 44 / 108 ms:

- Produce and Fetch call the broker's push and fetch as typed calls: the same commands, admission
  and reads as `POST /api/v1/push` and `POST /api/v1/fetch`, with no JSON in between. A test pins
  that both paths give byte-equal answers, so the two cannot drift apart unnoticed.
- A Kafka record travels as bytes in both directions.
- A topic created through Kafka gets `dedupWindowSeconds: 0`. A Kafka record carries no Queen
  `transactionId`, so the window could only ever check a brand-new id; Kafka's own duplicate
  suppression, the idempotent producer's sequence window, does not need it.
- Produce requests that a connection already has buffered are written as one push and answered in
  order. `QUEEN_KAFKA_COALESCE` (default 5, Kafka's `max.in.flight.requests.per.connection`) caps
  how many, and nothing waits for a request that has not arrived.

On the final build, Kafka Streams (`WordCountDemo`) and Kafka Connect in distributed mode, with a
file source into a topic and a file sink out of it, passed four runs out of four on fresh
three-node clusters.

## Still open

Overload was the first open problem. On 2.0.0-beta.5, at 2M msg/s offered, the facade accepted up
to 1.9M a second and its consumers starved: 105,000 consumed in the archived run, and anywhere
from 42,000 to 1,550,000 in repeats. 2.3.0 reads a fetch only to what its response can carry (a
consumer behind on a thousand partitions had a gigabyte read for the 32 MiB a response holds, on
every fetch), and at the same rate consumption now keeps up. What is left is the two late load
processes of the [2.3.0 run](#230-five-points): some consumers fall behind for the whole run while
the rest stay near 300 ms, and we do not know why. The fetch change itself has no before and
after measurement on one set of machines.

Long runs are the second, and have not been repeated on 2.3.0. A 10-minute run at 1M msg/s (`qk3`, 2026-10-01) moved 595 million
messages in and out with no errors, and its last 30 seconds held 51 / 111 ms. Three stretches of 20
to 100 seconds, around minutes 3, 6 and 8, had produce p99 of 2 to 8 seconds and put the run's e2e
p99 at 10 to 12 seconds. The first began when free memory on the nodes ran out and the followers
fell into direct reclaim, while each node's anonymous memory grew by about 13 GB over the ten
minutes.

The facade is also outside the [Jepsen](/benchmarks/jepsen/) tests, which drive the native HTTP
API; it has a compatibility suite of its own.

How to point a Kafka client at Queen, and what the facade supports, is in the
[Kafka guide](/guides/kafka/).
