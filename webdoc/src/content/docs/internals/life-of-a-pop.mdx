A pop answers one question: *which offsets may this consumer group take from which partitions right
now*. In 2.0 that answer is planned on the leader like any other command, and it takes effect as a
cursor write (a `CursorSet` effect) in an entry of the replicated log. Only after that entry has
applied on the node that took the request does the node read the payloads, from its own queue logs.
Everything around the claim is scheduling: choosing which partitions to ask about, and deciding
whether to wait.

## The three shapes of a pop

| Route | Scope | Command |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/v1/pop/queue/:queue` | across the queue's partitions | `PopWildcard` |
| `GET /api/v1/pop/queue/:queue/partition/:partition` | one named partition | `PopPinned` |
| `GET /api/v1/pop?namespace=&task=` | across every queue whose namespace and task match | `PopDiscover` |

All three funnel into one claim function per partition (`claim_one` in
`server/src/rsm/planner/pop.rs`); only candidate selection differs. A discovery pop needs at least one
of `namespace` or `task`: a bare `GET /api/v1/pop` is a `400`, not a scan of every queue.

The request parameters and their defaults: `batch` 200; `partitions` 1, clamped to 1 to 64;
`autoAck` false; `wait` false; `timeout` from `DEFAULT_TIMEOUT`, else `POP_DEFAULT_TIMEOUT_MS`, else
30000 ms, and never more than 60000 ms; `consumerGroup` defaults to the queue-mode group
`__QUEUE_MODE__`; `subscriptionMode` defaults to `DEFAULT_SUBSCRIPTION_MODE`. The lease length is
`leaseSeconds` when the request sets it, otherwise the queue's `leaseTime` (60 seconds on a queue a
push created); a discovery pop, which spans queues, uses 60 seconds unless the request sets it. The
worker id is a UUIDv7 minted per request, and on a leased pop it is the `leaseId` the client gets
back.

When pop maintenance mode is on, every pop answers at once without claiming anything.

## Step 1: the empty fast path

Most pops of an idle queue find nothing, and a plan costs a slot in the leader's serial pipeline. So
a wildcard pop first checks committed state on its own node: if the queue exists, the group is
registered, the group does not conflate, and no `pending` row of the group is ready, the pop is
provably empty and never becomes a command (`QUEEN_RAFT_POP_FASTPATH_EMPTY`, on by default). A push
that lands afterwards re-arms the partition and wakes the parked consumer, so nothing is stranded.

In a cluster that check reads the local node, which may not yet have applied an ack or a push another
node already answered. A pop that waits is woken when that entry applies here. A pop that does not
wait takes the read barrier first and looks again before it answers empty.

## Step 2: the command and its candidates

The receiver builds a `PopCommand` with the budget (`batch`), the width (`partitions`), the lease
length, the worker id and a planning deadline, and submits it. On the leader, the planner picks the
partitions to try:

- **A pinned pop** tries its one partition. An unknown partition answers empty and is never created:
  only a push creates a partition.
- **A wildcard pop** tries the group's **ready ring**, in FIFO order, then any partition of the queue
  appended to earlier in the same cycle. The ring is derived from the `pending` keyspace, which apply
  maintains: one row per `(tenant, queue, group, partition)` with work, carrying `ready_at`, never
  later than the earliest time that partition could yield a claim. A partition is ready when
  `ready_at` has passed, so a lease expiring or a `delayedProcessing` deadline passing re-arms a
  partition with no extra write. The ring is coarse by contract: every candidate is re-checked
  against its cursor row.
- **A discovery pop** runs the wildcard walk over each matching queue, sharing one budget.

The first wildcard or discovery pop of a group on a queue registers the group (a `GroupUpsert`
effect carrying its subscription mode, instant and conflation policy). A wildcard pop on a queue that
does not exist yet creates it with the implicit defaults, so a group can register before the first
push. A group registered with backlog to find (`all`, or a timestamp) has no `pending` rows yet for
the partitions that predate it, so that first wildcard pop tries every partition of the queue instead
of the ring. A `new` group has no backlog and rides the ring from the start.

The walk stops once `partitions` partitions have been claimed or the `batch` budget is spent. The
budget is shared: the first partition may take all of it.

## Step 3: visibility gates

Before it claims, `claim_one` reads two settings from the queue's configuration.

**`windowBuffer` (seconds)** is a quiet-period debounce: if the partition's newest append is younger
than `W` seconds, it delivers nothing, so a burst becomes one batch.

**`delayedProcessing` (seconds)** makes only appends at least `D` seconds old visible. Because append
timestamps are monotone within a partition, the filter cuts a contiguous tail, and the scan stops at
the first append that is too young.

## Step 4: seeding the cursor on first contact

The first time a `(partition, group)` pair is claimed, its cursor has to be seeded. An existing cursor
is never re-seeded.

- A **registered group** seeds by its stored policy: `all` just below the oldest retained offset
  (deliver everything retained); `new` just before the first append stamped at or after the group's
  registration; a timestamp subscription just before the first append at or after that timestamp.
  When nothing is that recent, the cursor lands on the tail. Because the comparison is by timestamp,
  a partition created after the group registered still delivers its whole backlog.
- A **pinned pop of an unregistered group** seeds from the intent it carries: `subscriptionFrom` as a
  timestamp, `new` or `subscriptionFrom=now` at the tail, and otherwise everything retained. A plain
  pinned pop does not register the group.
- The queue-mode group always starts from everything retained.

Apart from two cases (the empty-partition seal below, and the first pinned pop of a conflating group,
which registers the group and seeds every partition of the queue at once), a first contact that
delivers nothing writes no cursor: the seed is stable, so the next poll computes the same value, and
an idle poll costs no log entry.

## Step 5: the claim

With `wanted = committed + 1`, the claim works on the partition's append boundaries, which the
planner rebuilds from the replicated `txns` rows (base offset, end offset, timestamp) plus the appends
still in flight. It never reads a payload or a file position.

- **A live lease skips the partition.** Another worker's live lease (or this worker's) means nothing
  is claimed here this time; other partitions still serve. A lease granted under an earlier leader,
  timed by another node's clock, is held `QUEEN_RAFT_MAX_CLOCK_SKEW_MS` (500 ms) past its expiry
  before its batch goes to another worker. A lease that ran out with no ack is redelivered.
- **The head probe** takes the append whose range covers `wanted` and serves from `wanted` to its end,
  up to the budget.
- **The forward scan** then takes later appends in order, each from its first frame, until the budget
  is spent or an append is too young.
- **A retention gap is not an error.** If `wanted` is below the oldest retained offset, the batch
  starts at the first retained append.
- **The empty-partition seal.** If the scan takes nothing and the partition holds no live append at
  all while `last_offset > committed`, the cursor is sealed to `last_offset`. Without it, a partition
  whose appends were all removed by retention would stay a candidate for ever. It never fires when an
  append exists but is deferred by `delayedProcessing`.
- **A conflating group** takes one backward step instead: it serves the newest visible frame and
  leases the whole span up to it.

The planner also refuses to claim for a request nobody can answer: when the request's deadline falls
within `QUEEN_RAFT_POP_REPLY_MARGIN_MS` (50 ms) of the planner's clock, the pop answers empty rather
than lease messages no one will receive.

## Step 6: auto-ack or lease

This is the only branch that changes the delivery contract, and it is one `CursorSet` effect either
way.

**`autoAck=true`** moves `committed` to the last delivered offset in the same entry, holds no lease,
and returns an empty `leaseId`. If the consumer dies after the response is written but before the
work is done, those messages are gone. This is at-most-once.

**Otherwise the batch is leased.** The cursor row gets the worker, `batch_end` (the last delivered
offset), `lease_expires_at = now + lease seconds`, and the **delivered set**: the distinct
transaction-id hashes of the delivered messages, which the ack fast path later compares against. The
cursor does not move; only an ack moves it. `attempt_count` increases when a batch starts at the same
offset as the previous delivery (a nack or a lease expiry left the cursor there) and resets to 1
otherwise. It is telemetry and never spends the retry budget, which only an explicit `failed` ack
charges.

One cursor row holds at most one lease, so exactly one leased batch is in flight per
`(partition, group)`. A single-partition queue cannot be consumed in parallel by one group, however
many consumers run. Parallelism comes from partitions.

## Step 7: the answer, and waiting

When the entry applies on this node, the receiver reads each claimed range from its own queue log
(the index lookup of `(pid, offset)`, the record decompressed, the payload decrypted when the queue is
encrypted) and renders the messages. On a follower serving its own client, the leader plans the
claim and the follower waits until it has applied that entry before it reads; the follower asks the
leader to stop claiming 250 ms early (at most a quarter of the time left), because it still has to
apply and render.

A claim whose requester is gone is not left to expire. When the entry carrying a leased claim applies
and the pop that asked for it is no longer waiting (its deadline passed or the request was dropped),
the batcher releases the lease with a nack: the lease is dropped, the cursor stays, and no retry is
charged. The partition is claimable again within a cycle. A follower does the same for a claim it
could not deliver in time.

An empty result answers **`204` with no body at all**, unless the answer has something to say about
conflation.

If the result is empty, `wait=true`, and the deadline has not passed, a pinned or wildcard pop parks
on its own gate, one per `(tenant, queue, group)`. Apply wakes one parked pop of a group when a
partition becomes claimable for that group (an append, a lease released); a pinned pop is woken by
appends to its queue. A parked pop also re-checks on its own every `QUEEN_RAFT_POP_PARK_RECHECK_MS`
(5000 ms), the safety net for work that becomes claimable by the clock alone (a lease expiring, a
delayed message maturing). A re-poll that would start with less than `QUEEN_RAFT_POP_SUBMIT_MIN_MS`
(100 ms) left answers empty instead. A discovery pop does not park: it answers at once.

## Pop autopilot

A wildcard pop that sends `autopilot=true` and leaves `partitions` or `batch` unset hands those
dimensions to the broker; a value the client sends is never overridden. The choice is node-local and
made before planning, so the plan stays a pure function of the command. The width is the group's
partitions ready now, divided among this node's pops of that group in flight, clamped to 1 to 64. The
batch is drain-aware: the group's measured drain rate (messages acknowledged per second of lease)
times `QUEEN_RAFT_AUTOPILOT_DRAIN_MS` (200 ms), clamped to `QUEEN_RAFT_AUTOPILOT_BATCH_MIN` (100) and
`QUEEN_RAFT_AUTOPILOT_BATCH_MAX` (1000); a group with no measurement yet starts at the minimum. The
choice is echoed in the body as `"autopilot":{"partitions":W,"batch":B}`. A conflating pop is exempt.
The wire contract is on [pop autopilot](/reference/http/pop#autopilot).

## What to expect when things go wrong

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Another worker holds the lease | that partition is skipped this time; other partitions still serve |
| The consumer never acks | the lease expires and the batch is redelivered, `attempt_count` up, retry budget untouched |
| The requester is gone when the claim applies | the lease is released at once with a nack |
| Cursor below the oldest retained offset | the claim starts at the first retained append; not an error |
| Partition emptied entirely by retention | the cursor is sealed to `last_offset`; it stops being a candidate |
| No leader, or leadership lost | `503` with `Retry-After: 1` |
