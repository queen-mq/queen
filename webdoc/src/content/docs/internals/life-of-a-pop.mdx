A pop answers one question: *which offsets may this consumer group take from which partitions right
now*. In 2.0 that answer comes from the **consumption engine**, which runs on the leader and holds
every consumer group's cursors and leases in memory. A pop never becomes a log entry of its own. The
engine claims in memory, and the cursor rows the claim changed reach the replicated log in the
engine's next **checkpoint**, a few milliseconds later. The answer goes out only once that checkpoint
has committed, so every lease a consumer holds is already in the log. The node that took the request
then reads the payloads from its own queue logs. Everything around the claim is scheduling: which
partitions are ready, and how long to wait.

## The three shapes of a pop

| Route | Scope | Command |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/v1/pop/queue/:queue` | across the queue's partitions | `PopWildcard` |
| `GET /api/v1/pop/queue/:queue/partition/:partition` | one named partition | `PopPinned` |
| `GET /api/v1/pop?namespace=&task=` | across every queue whose namespace and task match | `PopDiscover` |

All three funnel into one claim function per partition (`claim_one` in
`server/src/rsm/consume/pop.rs`); only candidate selection differs. A discovery pop needs at least one
of `namespace` or `task`: a bare `GET /api/v1/pop` is a `400`, not a scan of every queue.

  N->>Q: read the claimed records, decompress, decrypt
  N-->>C: 200 with messages, or 204

The request parameters and their defaults: `batch` 200; `partitions` 1, clamped to 1 to 64 (on a
conflating pop it defaults to `batch`, since each partition yields at most one message); `autoAck`
false; `wait` false; `timeout` from `DEFAULT_TIMEOUT`, else `POP_DEFAULT_TIMEOUT_MS`, else 30000 ms, and
never more than 60000 ms; `consumerGroup` defaults to the queue-mode group `__QUEUE_MODE__`;
`subscriptionMode` defaults to `DEFAULT_SUBSCRIPTION_MODE`. The lease length is `leaseSeconds` when the
request sets it, otherwise the queue's `leaseTime` (60 seconds on a queue a push created); a discovery
pop, which spans queues, uses 60 seconds unless the request sets it. The worker id is a UUIDv7 minted
per request, and on a leased pop it is the `leaseId` the client gets back.

## Step 1: the command reaches the leader

The receiving node builds a `PopCommand` with the budget (`batch`), the width (`partitions`), the lease
length, the worker id and a deadline. Only the leader's engine serves it, and only while its **leader
lease** holds: the replicator must have heard a quorum acknowledge this node within
`QUEEN_CONSUME_LEADER_LEASE_MS` (400 ms; a single node always qualifies). Anywhere else the engine
answers `Retry`.

On a follower (`QUEEN_RAFT_CLIENT_OFFLOAD`, on by default in a cluster) the follower forwards the
prepared command to the leader, on a task of its own, with the time its caller has left. Pops skip the
forward window pushes queue in: their bytes are few, and a consumer stuck behind a queue of pushes
stops consuming. On the leader, one intake (`server/src/rsm/facade/intake.rs`) hands pops, acks,
nacks, renews and positional acks to the engine and everything else to the batcher, for its own
clients and for the followers' alike. In a cluster, a `Retry` (no leader yet, a leader
change) is taken to whoever leads now, under the same request id, until the deadline; when the
deadline passes the pop answers `503` with `Retry-After: 1`.

The engine runs on its own clock, so the leader recomputes the pop's deadline there from the time the
caller has left, less a margin to answer in: 250 ms, capped at a quarter of the time left. A claim is
answered only after its checkpoint commits, and a follower still has to apply and render what the
leader claimed, so every pop, the leader's own clients' too, stops claiming that much early. When the
deadline is within `QUEEN_RAFT_POP_REPLY_MARGIN_MS` (50 ms) of the engine's clock, the engine answers
empty rather than lease messages no one will receive.

## Step 2: the group, in memory

The engine keeps, for each consumer group, one **part** per partition: the cursor row (committed
offset, lease fields, delivered set, attempt counters) plus scheduling state. Parts are sharded by
partition id over 16 shards, one lock each. Per group and shard a **ready list** holds the parts that
can yield a claim now, oldest ready first; per shard a timer heap holds the instants at which a lease
expires or a visibility hold runs out; a per-worker index holds the live leases a renew extends.

All of it is loaded lazily from committed state. A group's first contact on this leader reads the
queue's configuration and the group's row. The first wildcard or discovery pop of the group loads the
whole queue: every partition becomes a part, its head and cursor row read from the store off the
request threads in chunks of 1024, while the pop that started the load waits. A pinned pop loads only
its partition. A partition created later joins every group that holds its whole queue when its
creation applies.

A part is **armed** (put on its ready list) when an append moves the partition's tail past the cursor,
when its lease is released or expires, and when a hold runs out. Apply reports every append and every
catalog effect to the leader's engine, so an append arms the part in the same step that makes the
message visible. A leased part is never on the ready list: its expiry waits on the timer heap.

The first wildcard or discovery pop of a group on a queue registers the group (a `GroupUpsert`
carrying its subscription mode, instant and conflation policy). A wildcard pop on a queue that does
not exist yet creates it with the implicit defaults (a `QueueUpsert`), so a group can register before
the first push. Both are catalog effects written by the engine's next checkpoint, and the pop's
answer waits for them to commit. A plain pinned pop does not register the group.

## Step 3: the candidates

- **A pinned pop** tries its one partition. An unknown partition answers empty and is never created:
  only a push creates a partition.
- **A wildcard pop** takes the group's ready parts shard by shard, from a starting shard that rotates
  from one pop to the next, and each shard's ready list oldest first. A part taken off the list that
  yields nothing waits for its next event.
- **A discovery pop** runs the wildcard walk over each matching queue, sharing one budget and one
  width.

The walk stops once `partitions` partitions have been claimed or the `batch` budget is spent. The
budget is shared: the first partition may take all of it.

## Step 4: visibility gates

Before it claims, `claim_one` reads two settings from the queue's configuration.

**`windowBuffer` (seconds)** is a quiet-period debounce: if the partition's newest append is younger
than `W` seconds, it delivers nothing, so a burst becomes one batch. The part is held until the newest
append is `W` seconds old.

**`delayedProcessing` (seconds)** makes only appends at least `D` seconds old visible. Because append
timestamps are monotone within a partition, the filter cuts a contiguous tail, and the scan stops at
the first append that is too young. A part that yields nothing because of it is held until that append
matures.

A hold is a timer on the part's shard: when it runs out, the part is armed again with no poll and no
write.

## Step 5: seeding the cursor on first contact

The first time a `(partition, group)` pair is claimed, its cursor has to be seeded. An existing cursor
is never re-seeded.

- A **registered group** seeds by its stored policy: `all` just below the oldest retained offset
  (deliver everything retained); `new` just before the first append stamped at or after the group's
  registration; a timestamp subscription just before the first append at or after that timestamp.
  When nothing is that recent, the cursor lands on the tail. Because the comparison is by timestamp,
  a partition created after the group registered still delivers its whole backlog. An instant still in
  the future is evaluated again at every claim until it passes.
- A **pinned pop of an unregistered group** seeds from the intent it carries: `subscriptionFrom` as a
  timestamp, `new` or `subscriptionFrom=now` at the tail, and otherwise everything retained. A plain
  pinned pop does not register the group.
- The queue-mode group always starts from everything retained.

Apart from two cases (the empty-partition seal below, and the first pinned pop of a conflating group,
which registers the group and seeds every partition of the queue at once), a first contact that
delivers nothing writes no cursor row: the seed is stable, so the next poll computes the same value,
and an idle poll costs nothing in the log.

## Step 6: the claim

With `wanted = committed + 1`, the claim works on the partition's append boundaries: base offset, end
offset, timestamp and the transaction hash of every frame, read from the replicated `txns` rows (from
the queue log instead under `QUEEN_RAFT_DEDUP_INDEX=segment`). It never reads a payload or a file
position.

- **A live lease skips the partition.** Another worker's live lease (or this worker's) means nothing
  is claimed here this time; other partitions still serve. A lease granted under an earlier leader,
  timed by another node's clock, is held `QUEEN_RAFT_MAX_CLOCK_SKEW_MS` (500 ms) past its expiry
  before its batch goes to another worker. A lease that ran out with no ack is redelivered.
- **The head probe** takes the append whose range covers `wanted` and serves from `wanted` to its end,
  up to the budget.
- **The forward scan** then takes later appends in order, each from its first frame, until the budget
  is spent or an append is too young.
- **A retention gap is not an error.** If `wanted` is below the oldest retained offset, the batch
  starts at the first retained append.
- **The empty-partition seal.** If the scan takes nothing and the partition holds no live append at
  all while `last_offset > committed`, the cursor is sealed to `last_offset`. Without it, a partition
  whose appends were all removed by retention would stay a candidate for ever. It never fires when an
  append exists but is deferred by `delayedProcessing`.
- **A conflating group** takes one backward step instead: it serves the newest visible frame and
  leases the whole span up to it.

## Step 7: auto-ack or lease

This is the only branch that changes the delivery contract, and it ends in one cursor row either way.

**`autoAck=true`** moves `committed` to the last delivered offset, holds no lease, and returns an
empty `leaseId`. The answer still waits for the checkpoint that writes the moved cursor, so a leader
that fails afterwards cannot deliver those messages again. If the consumer dies after the response is
written but before the work is done, those messages are gone. This is at-most-once.

**Otherwise the batch is leased.** The part's cursor gets the worker, `batch_end` (the last delivered
offset), `lease_expires_at = now + lease seconds` on the engine's clock, and the **delivered set**:
the distinct transaction-id hashes of the delivered messages, which the ack fast path later compares
against. The engine also keeps the hash of every leased offset in memory, so an ack resolves without
a store read. The cursor does not move; only an ack moves it. `attempt_count` increases when a batch
starts at the same offset as the previous delivery (a nack or a lease expiry left the cursor there)
and resets to 1 otherwise. It is telemetry and never spends the retry budget, which only an explicit
`failed` ack charges.

One cursor holds at most one lease, so exactly one leased batch is in flight per
`(partition, group)`. A single-partition queue cannot be consumed in parallel by one group, however
many consumers run. Parallelism comes from partitions.

## Step 8: the checkpoint

A claim, an ack or a seal marks its part changed. Every `QUEEN_CONSUME_CHECKPOINT_MS` (5 ms) the
engine's ticker gathers every part changed since the last checkpoint into **whole** `CursorSet` rows,
with the dead letters an ack filed beside them: one `Effects` command per tenant and planner lane
(`lane = pid % QUEEN_LANES`, 8 by default; at most `QUEEN_CONSUME_ROWS_PER_COMMAND`, 4096, rows each),
plus one command per tenant for the catalog effects (registrations, a pop's implicit queue). They go to
the batcher like any other command and ride the next entry of the replicated log, and the batcher
reports each one as soon as its entry commits.

Every answer lists the rows it depends on, at the version it saw. It goes out once all of them are
durable, so one checkpoint releases every claim and ack of those 5 ms together. A part whose previous
row is still in flight waits for the following checkpoint, so one row is never in two entries at
once. A checkpoint that fails leaves its parts marked, and the next one writes their state as it is
then. `QUEEN_CONSUME_FAST=1` answers before the checkpoint instead, and a claim answered that way can
be handed out again if the leader fails before its row commits.

Apply writes the rows on every node like any other effect. On the leader it reports them to the
engine, which skips the rows of its own term (it wrote them) and adopts any other cursor row, such as
an earlier leader's checkpoint applied late.

## Step 9: the answer, and waiting

When the claims come back, the receiving node reads each claimed range from its own queue log (the
index lookup of `(pid, offset)`, the record decompressed, the payload decrypted when the queue is
encrypted) and renders the messages. On a follower, the leader's reply carries the index the leader
had applied when it answered, and the follower waits until it has applied that far itself, so every
claimed append is in its own queue logs before it reads. If it cannot get there before its caller's
deadline, it hands the leases back, and its next pop first waits until it has applied that far,
answering `503` if that does not happen in time. A node that fell behind stops taking partitions it
cannot serve, and the one it released goes to a consumer of a node that can.

A claim whose requester is gone is not left to expire. When the engine's answer finds no one waiting
for it (the deadline passed, the request was dropped, the follower's caller left), the leases it
granted are released at once: the lease is dropped, the cursor stays, no retry is charged, and the
part is armed again.

An empty result answers **`204` with no body at all**, unless the answer has something to say about
conflation.

A long-poll (`wait=true`) that finds nothing is **held on the leader**, in its group, whichever node
the consumer is attached to. When a part of the group is armed, the group is queued for the engine's
serve thread, which runs the group's held pops again, oldest first, until one finds nothing. A pinned
pop waits on its partition. A held pop is answered empty when its deadline, less the 50 ms margin,
comes: a heap of deadlines makes that a lookup, not a sweep of every waiting group. A discovery pop is
never held: it answers at once.

An empty answer that the engine did not hold comes back early (the queue does not exist yet, the
group was just dropped). The receiving node then waits on its own gate for that
`(tenant, queue, group)`, woken by the engine or by an append, for at most 250 ms (or
`QUEEN_RAFT_POP_PARK_RECHECK_MS`, when that is lower), and asks again. A re-poll that would start with
less than `QUEEN_RAFT_POP_SUBMIT_MIN_MS` (100 ms) left answers empty instead.

## When the leader changes

A node that begins to lead forgets whatever its engine held before and loads groups lazily again, from
the checkpointed rows. The leases in those rows stay live, with the skew grace above, so a batch
answered by the previous leader is not handed to another worker before it expires. For the checkpoint
interval plus the clock skew (505 ms by default) it serves nothing, which covers answers and leases the
previous leader may still have given; pops that arrive meanwhile are held, not refused, and served when
the pause ends. A single voter has had no other leader and does not pause.

The engine's clock is the monotonic clock, anchored when the node begins to lead at the latest of the
wall clock, the log's own clock and what the engine read before. Every lease, deadline and hold is
timed on it, so a wall clock that jumps while a node leads cannot cut a lease short.

A node that stops leading answers every pop it holds with `Retry`, and the caller's node takes it to
whoever leads now. A claim whose checkpoint had not committed was never answered, so its messages are
simply claimable again under the new leader.

## Pop autopilot

A wildcard pop that sends `autopilot=true` and leaves `partitions` or `batch` unset hands those
dimensions to the broker; a value the client sends is never overridden. The **width** is chosen by the
leader's engine inside the claim, from exact state: the group's partitions ready now, divided among
the group's pops held on the leader plus this one, rounded up and clamped to 1 to 64. A pop a follower
forwards is sized the same way. The **batch** is chosen by the receiving node before it submits, and
is drain-aware: the group's drain rate measured on that node (messages acknowledged per second of
lease) times `QUEEN_RAFT_AUTOPILOT_DRAIN_MS` (200 ms), clamped to `QUEEN_RAFT_AUTOPILOT_BATCH_MIN`
(100) and `QUEEN_RAFT_AUTOPILOT_BATCH_MAX` (1000); a group with no measurement yet starts at the
minimum. The choice is echoed in the body as `"autopilot":{"partitions":W,"batch":B}`; a delegated
`W` reports the partitions the pop claimed (1 when it claimed none). A conflating pop is exempt. The
wire contract is on [pop autopilot](/reference/http/pop#autopilot).

## What to expect when things go wrong

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Another worker holds the lease | that partition is skipped this time; other partitions still serve |
| The consumer never acks | the lease expires on the engine's timer and the batch is redelivered, `attempt_count` up, retry budget untouched |
| The requester is gone when the answer is ready | the leases are released at once: cursor unmoved, no retry charged |
| A follower cannot apply the claimed messages before its caller's deadline | it releases the claims and sends no pop until it has caught up; its callers get `503` |
| Cursor below the oldest retained offset | the claim starts at the first retained append; not an error |
| Partition emptied entirely by retention | the cursor is sealed to `last_offset`; it stops being a candidate |
| The leader changes | answered leases stay held; pops wait out the new leader's pause, then are served |
| No leader, or the leader lost its quorum | the pop is retried until its deadline, then `503` with `Retry-After: 1` |
