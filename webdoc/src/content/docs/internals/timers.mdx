A timer is a message that is not in its queue's log yet. It waits as a row in the `timers`
keyspace, and the log sees it only when it fires: one replicated entry appends the message and
deletes the row, so on every node both happen or neither does.

<Aside type="note">
Every node serves the timer routes: `POST /api/v1/timers`, `GET /api/v1/timers/:queue`, and `GET`
and `DELETE` on `/api/v1/timers/:queue/*timerKey`. Only the leader fires. The operator's side is on
[KV and timers](/deploy/state).
</Aside>

## Why a timer waits outside the log

A pop claims a partition's messages in offset order from its cursor and stops at the first one that
is not deliverable yet. A message due next week at the head of that range would hold every later
message of its partition until then. And a queue log is append-only, reclaimed only by retention
watermarks, so a cancel would have nothing to remove. So the message is kept as a row, which a
cancel can delete, and it enters the log at the moment it is due.

## Two keyspaces

| Keyspace | Key | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `timers` | `(tenant, queue, timer_key)` | the timer row |
| `timers_due` | `(due, tenant, queue, timer_key)` | nothing: this is the fire order |

`due` is the delivery time, or a later instant a backoff set. The fire walks `timers_due` from its
front, so finding what is due costs the timers that fire, not the timers that wait.

The key is made of names. There is no partition id in it, because the destination queue and
partition are created at the fire, the way a push creates them, and not at the schedule. Two
consequences follow: scheduling a timer never provisions or validates its destination, and deleting
a queue leaves the timers that name it pending; when one fires, its push creates the queue again.

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `partition` | The destination partition name, `Default` when the schedule names none. |
| `deliver_at` | The planner's instant plus `delayMs`. A floor, never an appointment: the message is not delivered earlier. A delay in the past is legal and fires in the first cycle. |
| `visible_at` | Set only by a backoff: the timer is not due before it. |
| `frame` | The message exactly as the fire's push will carry it. The receiver decodes the base64 payload, decompresses it when `payloadZstd` is set, encrypts it when the destination queue encrypts at rest, and packs the frame at schedule time, so the fire only appends. |
| `txn` | The fixed transaction id of the future message. Required. |
| `message_id` | Minted at schedule time, so the schedule answers with the id the delivery will carry. |
| `attempts`, `last_error` | Permanent fire failures only. |
| `producer_sub` | The authenticated subject of whoever scheduled. A client cannot supply it, nor a message id or a tenant: those fields are refused. |
| `created_at`, `updated_at` | `created_at` survives a reschedule. |

Encryption is decided at schedule time: a timer scheduled before its queue started encrypting at
rest is delivered as it was packed.

## Scheduling and cancelling

A timers call (`POST /api/v1/timers`, or the `DELETE` route for one cancel) is one command, planned
serially and answered once its entry has committed and applied on the node that took it. A
`(queue, timerKey)` may appear once per call. Scheduling a key that already has a pending timer is a
reschedule: a new timer under the old name, with the attempts reset, the error cleared, and the
`txn`, message id and frame replaced. A call whose every op is a cancel of an absent timer logs
nothing. `POST /api/v1/transaction` carries the same ops in its `timers` array, planned into the
transaction's entry beside its pushes, acks and KV writes; that route goes straight to the state
machine and does not consult the runtime switches or the quotas.

The peek, list and count routes read the node's own state after waiting until it has applied what
the cluster had committed when the read began.

## The fire

The leader's batcher runs one fire step every `QUEEN_RAFT_TIMER_TICK_MS` (default 50; `0` turns the
fire off) and also in any cycle that plans a timers command, so a timer scheduled already due fires
in the entry that schedules it. The first tick after the broker starts is not skipped: timers that
came due while it was down fire in the first cycle it plans.

Nothing else stops the fire. The `timersFire` runtime switch is stored and reported, but the fire
step does not read it: setting `QUEEN_RAFT_TIMER_TICK_MS=0` on every node, which takes a restart, is
the only way to stop firing.

One step:

<Steps>

1. **Takes the due timers**: `timers_due` from its front while `due` is at or before the step's
   instant, merged with the entries still in flight, whose view of a timer wins. At most
   `QUEEN_RAFT_TIMER_FIRE_BATCH` (default 256) timers and `QUEEN_RAFT_TIMER_FIRE_MAX_BYTES` (default
   4 MiB) of frames, earliest first. One timer always goes, so an oversized frame still fires.

2. **Pushes them**: grouped by tenant, queue and partition, each group is one push through the same
   planner a client push uses, with the same offsets, the same implicit creation of the queue and
   partition, and the same deduplication. Two timers of one group that share a `txn` go in separate
   pushes, so the second is judged against the first.

3. **Settles each timer**: appended, or a duplicate (its `txn` is already in the destination
   partition's deduplication window), and the timer is deleted; or the push was refused, and the
   timer backs off or is dead-lettered (next section).

</Steps>

The step's effects are one command with its own request id, added to the cycle's entry and answered
by nobody. The appended message and the timer's removal are in the same entry, so they commit, apply
and replay together: there is no claim, no lease, and nothing to reconcile after a crash. A step
that hit one of its bounds runs again in the next cycle instead of waiting for the tick.

The step is planned after the cycle's client commands, so a cancel or a reschedule taken in the same
cycle wins over the fire. A timer whose fire is in an entry still in flight is already gone as far
as the planner is concerned, so a cancel planned after it answers `absent`. The cancel lands before
the fire or after it; there is no third outcome and no `too_late` verdict.

## Backoff and the dead letter

When the fire's push is refused:

- a **transient** refusal backs the timer off by `QUEEN_SWEEPER_TRANSIENT_BACKOFF_MS` (default
  1000) and spends no attempt, so infrastructure trouble never uses up a timer's budget;
- a **permanent** refusal counts an attempt and backs off by `QUEEN_SWEEPER_BACKOFF_MIN_MS`, doubled
  for each earlier permanent failure and capped at `QUEEN_SWEEPER_BACKOFF_MAX_MS` (defaults 1000 and
  60000 ms);
- once permanent failures reach `QUEEN_SWEEPER_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (default 5), the timer is
  dead-lettered in the same entry: the destination queue and partition are created if missing, a
  dead letter carrying the payload is filed under the consumer group `__timer__` at offset `-1`, and
  the timer is deleted.

A backoff is a `TimerBackoff` effect: a new visibility, the attempt count and the error. The row
stays pending and can still be cancelled. If the dead letter itself cannot be planned, the timer
backs off instead and the next attempt tries again. A value of 0 or below for any of the four knobs
is ignored; their names are the ones 1.x used.

## Delete, not mark done

The fire deletes the row. There is no status and no history of fired timers, so the two keyspaces
hold pending work only. "Was it sent?" is answered by the log: look for the timer's `txn` in the
destination queue.

The price: after the fire there is no tombstone, so a cancel answers `absent` and a peek
`found: false`. That verdict means no longer pending. It may mean delivered, which is why a cancel's
`absent` carries `ok: false` and echoes the `txn` the caller sent.

## Apply

`TimerUpsert` writes the row and its `timers_due` entry, replacing the old entry when the row
existed. `TimerDelete` removes both. `TimerBackoff` rewrites the row and moves its `timers_due`
entry to the new instant. A missing row is a counted no-op, so a replayed entry converges. Apply
reads no clock: `deliver_at` and every backoff instant travel in the effect. Deleting a tenant
removes its timers in the purge entry.
