There is no sweeper task on a 2.0 node. Background work comes in two kinds, split by what it
changes:

- **Work that changes replicated state** is planned by the leader, in the same batcher cycle as the
  client commands, and travels in the replicated log like a client write. Every node applies the same
  effects, so a follower never sweeps on its own and two nodes never race over one row.
- **Work on one node's own files or memory** runs on every node, on its own clock, and never enters
  the log.

## Leader steps

| Step | Cadence | Bound per step | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Request-id expiry | every 10 s | 4096 outcomes | `RequestIdsExpire`: forgets the recorded outcomes older than `QUEEN_RAFT_REQUEST_ID_WINDOW_S` (default 60) |
| [KV expiry](/internals/kv) | `QUEEN_RAFT_KV_SWEEP_MS` (default 1000) | `QUEEN_RAFT_KV_SWEEP_LIMIT` keys (default 512) | one `KvDelete` per expired key |
| [Timer fire](/internals/timers) | `QUEEN_RAFT_TIMER_TICK_MS` (default 50; `0` never), and every cycle that plans a timers command | `QUEEN_RAFT_TIMER_FIRE_BATCH` timers (default 256) and `QUEEN_RAFT_TIMER_FIRE_MAX_BYTES` (default 4 MiB) | the pushes, then a `TimerDelete`, a `TimerBackoff` or a dead letter per timer |
| [Retention](/internals/retention) | `RETENTION_INTERVAL` ms (default 5000) | `RETENTION_BATCH_SIZE` effects (default 1000) | `Watermark`, `PartitionDelete`, `DeleteChunk`, `TraceExpire` |

The recorded outcomes are what let a command that was forwarded or retried after a leader change,
with the same request id, get the first attempt's answer instead of being applied twice.

What the four steps share:

- **Only the leader plans them**, and only while its pipeline can take another entry. On a follower
  the ticks plan nothing.
- **They are planned after the cycle's client commands**, against committed state plus the entries
  still in flight. A client write in the same cycle wins: a key it rewrote is not swept, a timer it
  cancelled does not fire, a partition it pushed to is not deleted.
- **Each step is one command** with its own request id, added to the cycle's entry and answered by
  nobody. It commits, applies and replays exactly like a client write.
- **A step that finds more work than its bound** runs again in the next cycle; that holds for
  request-id expiry, the fire and retention. The KV sweep waits for its next tick.
- **A cycle that cannot read the store** leaves the KV sweep, the fire and retention to their next
  tick; request-id expiry stays due.

## No lease sweep

Message leases expire without a background step. A claim judges a lease against the state machine's
clock, so a cursor whose lease has passed is claimable by the next pop. When a pop leases a
partition, apply records the partition as ready for that consumer group again at the lease's
expiry, which is how the next pop after that instant finds it.

## Node-local loops

| Loop | Cadence | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Queue-log reclaim | each `RETENTION_INTERVAL` tick, on every node | unlinks or rewrites the sealed queue-log files the watermarks made dead ([Retention](/internals/retention)); `QUEEN_QLOG_RECLAIM=false` turns it off |
| Queue-log idle pass | at most every 5 s, on the queue-log writer's thread when it writes | seals an active file whose data is older than `QUEEN_QLOG_SEAL_AGE_S` (default 600, `0` never) and removes the directory of a per-queue log with nothing left, at most 64 seals and removals per pass |
| Ephemeral backstop | once a second | expires leases, drops messages past their ttl and collects idle implicit queues on the ephemeral rings nobody touched; a ring does the same on every touch, so a busy queue needs nothing from it |
| Dashboard collector | `METRICS_FLUSH_MS` (default 60000) | writes this node's rows into `dash.db` ([Counters, rates and lag](/internals/stats)) |

Ephemeral queues keep their contents in one node's memory, outside the replicated log, which is why
their backstop is local.

## The timer backoff knobs

Four `QUEEN_SWEEPER_*` variables are still read, all by the fire step's backoff:
`QUEEN_SWEEPER_BACKOFF_MIN_MS` (default 1000), `QUEEN_SWEEPER_BACKOFF_MAX_MS` (default 60000),
`QUEEN_SWEEPER_TRANSIENT_BACKOFF_MS` (default 1000) and `QUEEN_SWEEPER_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (default 5).
Their names come from 1.x; what they do is on [Timer internals](/internals/timers). No other
`QUEEN_SWEEPER_*` variable is read.
