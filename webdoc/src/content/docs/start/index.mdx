Queen MQ is a message broker written in Rust that keeps its data in PostgreSQL. It was built at
Smartness to solve head-of-line blocking in Smartchat, a messaging system for hotels where much
of the pipeline is slow and uneven: translations, agent calls. Everything a broker is expected to
have was added on top of that, and what came out is small enough to operate and complete enough
to keep.

The idea underneath is one sentence: an arbitrarily large number of ordered partitions, created
by the push that names them. That gives ordering per entity and no head-of-line blocking between
entities, at the same time.

Every architectural choice follows from four things: operational simplicity, observability,
delivery guarantees, performance. Queen exists for the places where complexity is the enemy. That
is why the transport is HTTP (curl is a Queen client), the broker is one stateless binary,
consumers long-poll, and the storage is PostgreSQL.

A full deployment has four parts:

- a Rust proxy, optional, that isolates the broker from the outside world and makes it
  multi-tenant
- the Rust broker, carrying its own PostgreSQL stored procedures
- one PostgreSQL, dedicated or shared with your other applications
- the clients, all of them over HTTP

<pre class="mermaid">{`
flowchart LR
  C["clients<br/>six SDKs, queenctl, curl"] -->|HTTP| P["Rust proxy<br/>optional: isolation, tenancy"]
  P -->|HTTP| B["Rust broker<br/>stateless, holds no durable state"]
  C -.->|HTTP, with no proxy| B
  B -->|stored procedures| PG[("PostgreSQL<br/>the source of truth")]
`}</pre>

Only the last box keeps anything. The broker can be restarted, scaled or rolled without a
rebalance because there is no state in it to move.

The `transactionId` is optional and is the idempotency key: give it your own and a retry of the
same push writes nothing the second time.

Queen has been run with a million dynamic partitions at 200,000 messages a second, and at a
million messages a second per side for 24 hours. Consumers choose how they read: which partition,
how many at once, batch size, consumer group, lease time.

<Render file="snippets/js-consume" />

`group` picks the cursor, `partitions` and `batch` how much one poll claims, `limit` and
`idleMillis` when the loop stops, `renewLease` how a long handler keeps its claim.

Delivery is at-least-once. Acknowledgement sits between a plain offset commit and a per-message
ack/nack, implicit or explicit, and one PostgreSQL transaction can carry the ack of a message and
the push of the next one together.

<Render file="snippets/js-transaction" />

Deduplication on push, plus ack-and-push in one transaction, is what makes a processing pipeline
reliable and complex mechanisms buildable on top of it.

Between the push, pop and ack options, one broker covers most shapes: high throughput, fan-out,
replay, request-reply.

Queen is multi-tenant, high throughput, high cardinality, and easy to operate.
