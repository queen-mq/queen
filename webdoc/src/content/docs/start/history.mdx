Queen was born in October 2025, to solve head-of-line blocking in Smartchat. That product used
Kafka as the backbone between its microservices, and kept hitting the same wall: the queues whose
processing time is long and uneven, translations and agent replies, block everything queued
behind them. The first fix was a workaround. Consume from Kafka, write the task to PostgreSQL,
run a loop that does the work, publish back to Kafka.

That is where the idea came from: generalise the workaround into a small C++ broker with
PostgreSQL procedures of its own. It worked, it was traceable, and it removed the problem, so the
next step was to give it Kafka's semantics as well: consumer groups and replay.

By November 2025 the first prototype had replaced Kafka across Smartchat. From there the work was
speed and scale, from the 30,000 messages a second of the early versions to the million of 1.x,
which arrived with a new Rust broker and a new PostgreSQL schema.

## Where 1.x runs

Queen 1.x is the engine of at least three products at Smartness, including both of the ones that
have to be real time. Between them they run about **60 queues, 100,000 partitions and 60 consumer
groups**, and that load sits at around **20% of a two-core PostgreSQL on GCP**: the cardinality
that this design makes cheap is exactly the cardinality that makes other brokers expensive.

The 1.x broker runs highly available, three replicas over that PostgreSQL. It holds no
coordination state, so the replicas need nothing from one another and a rolling restart is a
rolling restart, not a rebalance.

## 2.0: its own replicated log

2.0 removes PostgreSQL. The broker keeps its messages, cursors, key/value state and timers on its
own local disk, as a log of entries and the state those entries build, and a cluster of three or
five nodes replicates that log with Raft, so durability and failover are the broker's own rather
than a database's. Everything runs in one binary: the proxy that fronts tenants and the Kafka wire
facade run inside the broker process when they are switched on. The SQS facade and the S3 sink
were removed in the same step.

2.0 is in alpha: its first alpha was committed in September 2026.
