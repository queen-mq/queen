The end

Eight systems became one broker.

  • Retries and dead letters: a lease and an answer per message.
  • Outbox and idempotency table: one commit.
  • Reminders table and cron: timers.
  • Redis locks: a lock, and a guard on the commit.
  • Reply queues: ephemeral queues.
  • A stream processor and a search index: the feed.
  • Debezium and Connect: connectors in the broker.
Why

Each box was a system to run, and a seam where a step could come apart.

The end

Move one service at a time.

docker run -p 6632:6632 -p 127.0.0.1:9092:9092 \
  -v /var/lib/queen:/var/lib/queen/raft \
  -e QUEEN_KAFKA_EMBEDDED=true \
  -e QUEEN_KAFKA_ADVERTISED_ADDR=127.0.0.1:9092 \
  ghcr.io/queen-mq/queen:2.8.1
  • Kafka producers, consumers and admin tools work as they are: change bootstrap.servers, nothing else.
  • A topic is a queue, a Kafka consumer group is a QueenMQ group. Kafka clients and QueenMQ clients read and write the same data.
  • From RabbitMQ or SQS there is no port: move one service at a time with the SDKs.

The end

Build on QueenMQ, unless it is a firehose.

  • One leader orders all of a queue's writes, and more nodes do not raise that. Transactions too: about 12,900 a second per tenant on 16 vCPU.
  • Three 24-vCPU nodes, one queue of 1,000 partitions: QueenMQ carried 3,000,000 msg/s at a p99 of 15 ms, and not 5,500,000. Kafka carried about 8,000,000 at 12 ms, answering before any fsync.
  • Kafka's lead ends as partitions multiply: at 10,000 it used twice QueenMQ's CPU, and it did not carry 50,000.
Why

One leader per tenant is what makes a transaction one log entry. That is the trade: a ceiling, for steps that cannot come apart.
